SMART WAREHOUSE AND ORDER FULFILLMENT ERP SYSTEM

Student Contributors: Nachiket Jagdale (A7-20), Mayank Ruchandani (A7-15), Yash Jagyasi (A7-49)
Subject Coordinator: Kaushik R. Roy | Programme: E-commerce and ERP (Semester V)

1. Background & Problem Statement

In many small and medium businesses, inventory, purchasing, and order records exist in isolated files. This creates stock mismatches, missed replenishment cycles, and zero visibility into order fulfillment. Warehouse studies demonstrate that receiving, storage, picking, and shipping must function as an integrated workflow.

2. Literature Review & Research Gap Addressed

Author(s) & Year Title / Focus Key Contribution Addressed in our ERP
Gu et al. (2007) Warehouse Operations Reviews receiving, storage, picking, shipping. Integrated Single Workflow
de Koster et al. (2007) Order Picking Reviews storage layout, zoning, batching. Zone & Bin Matrix System
Masae et al. (2020) Picker Routing Picker routing optimization methods. Ordered Pick Lists by Aisle
Haddara & Zach (2011) ERP in SMEs Implementation hurdles for small enterprises. Lightweight Modular Web ERP

3. Implementation Architecture (Technology Stack)

  • Frontend: Modern Web ERP UI with responsive CSS Grid/Flexbox, real-time KPI reactive state, and modal management.
  • Backend API: Python with FastAPI, Pydantic schemas, and JWT role-based access (admin vs warehouse staff).
  • Database Engine: PostgreSQL relational schema mapping products, suppliers, warehouses, stock movements, and customer orders.
  • Order Workflow: Stock Check → Stock Reservation → Pick List → Packing Slip → Dispatch Manifest → Delivery Confirmation.
